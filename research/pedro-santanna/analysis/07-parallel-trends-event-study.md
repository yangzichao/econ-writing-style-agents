# Writing Style Analysis: The Role of Parallel Trends in Event Study Settings

## Paper Info
- Authors: Michelle Marcus, Pedro H. C. Sant'Anna
- ArXiv v1 date: September 3, 2020
- Published: Journal of the Association of Environmental and Resource Economists (JAERE), 2021

## Abstract Comparison
### v1 Abstract
"Difference-in-Differences (DID) research designs usually rely on variation of treatment timing such that, after making an appropriate parallel trends assumption, one can identify, estimate, and make inference about causal effects. In practice, however, different DID procedures rely on different parallel trends assumptions (PTA), and recover different causal parameters. In this paper, we focus on staggered DID (also referred as event-studies) and discuss the role played by the PTA in terms of identification and estimation of causal parameters. We document a 'robustness' vs. 'efficiency' trade-off in terms of the strength of the underlying PTA, and argue that practitioners should be explicit about these trade-offs whenever using DID procedures. We propose new DID estimators that reflect these trade-offs and derived their large sample properties. We illustrate the practical relevance of these results by assessing whether the transition from federal to state management of the Clean Water Act affects compliance rates."

### Final Abstract
The published abstract is essentially identical to the v1 abstract, with only minor typographic/encoding differences (e.g., "ff" ligatures rendered differently in the published PDF). The content, structure, and wording are unchanged.

## Writing Style Patterns Observed
- **Sentence Structure**: Consistent across both versions. The paper uses a mix of short declarative sentences and longer explanatory ones. There is no evolution to observe.
- **Vocabulary/Terminology**: Identical across versions. The paper consistently uses "PTA" as an abbreviation, "DID" (not "DiD"), and speaks of "staggered DID" and "event-studies."
- **Framing/Motivation**: The framing is identical: the paper is positioned as a "clarifying" contribution that helps researchers understand the implications of different PTAs.
- **Precision**: Equal precision in both versions. The stylized example with 4 time periods is presented identically.
- **Tone**: The tone is consistent -- accessible yet technically precise. Phrases like "To the best of our knowledge" appear identically in both versions.
- **Conciseness**: No change in length or density between versions.
- **Reader Engagement**: Both versions use the same pedagogical devices (stylized example, direct comparison of papers by abbreviation S&A, C&S, dC&D).

## Specific Revision Examples

This paper is notable for its lack of substantive revisions between v1 and publication. This could indicate several things:

### Example 1: Stability of the manuscript
The ArXiv v1 posting date (September 3, 2020) and the "First version" note (January 10, 2020) suggest the paper went through internal revisions before the ArXiv posting. The version posted to ArXiv was likely already the version submitted to the journal, or very close to it. JAERE's revision process may have resulted in only minor changes not visible at the level of text extracted from PDF.

### Example 2: Paper type
This is a shorter, more applied/expository paper compared to the other papers in this analysis. It serves as a guide for practitioners rather than a heavy theoretical contribution. Such papers may require fewer revisions because their scope is narrower and the arguments are more self-contained.

### Example 3: Co-author dynamics
Michelle Marcus is the first author (an environmental economist), with Sant'Anna providing the econometric methodology. The applied nature and clear empirical motivation may have helped the paper arrive at ArXiv in a polished state.

### Example 4: Consistent style markers
Despite the lack of revision, the paper exhibits distinctive Sant'Anna style markers:
- Use of "As so" as a transition phrase (e.g., "As so, policy evaluations based on TWFE linear regression models can be misleading")
- "To the best of our knowledge" before novel claims
- "It is worth stressing" and "It is worth mentioning" as emphasis markers
- References to "transparency and objectivity" in analysis, citing Rubin (2007, 2008)
- Advocacy for simultaneous (vs. pointwise) confidence bands

### Example 5: Formatting consistency
The only observable "changes" are encoding differences in the published PDF vs. ArXiv. For example, ligatures like "ff" in "difference" appear as distinct characters in one but not the other. This is a rendering artifact, not a writing choice. It confirms the text content is essentially identical.
