# Writing Style Analysis: Was Javert right to be suspicious? MTE with Duration Outcomes

## Paper Info
- Authors: Santiago Acerenza, Vitor Possebom, Pedro H. C. Sant'Anna
- ArXiv v1 date: November 23, 2023
- Latest draft date: January 8, 2025

## Abstract Comparison
### v1 Abstract
"This paper presents new econometric tools to unpack the treatment effect heterogeneity of punishing misdemeanor offenses on time-to-recidivism. We show how one can identify, estimate, and make inferences on the distributional, quantile, and average marginal treatment effects in setups where the treatment selection is endogenous and the outcome of interest, usually a duration variable, is potentially right-censored. We explore our proposed econometric methodology to evaluate the effect of fines and community service sentences as a form of punishment on time-to-recidivism in the State of Sao Paulo, Brazil, between 2010 and 2019, leveraging the as-if random assignment of judges to cases. Our results highlight substantial treatment effect heterogeneity that other tools are not meant to capture. For instance, we find that people who most judges would punish take longer to recidivate as a consequence of the punishment, while people who would be punished only by strict judges recidivate at an earlier date than if they were not punished. This result suggests that designing sentencing guidelines that encourage strict judges to become more lenient could reduce recidivism."

2. "people **who** most judges would punish" became "people **whom** most judges would punish" -- corrected grammar (objective case).

The final sentence of the v1 abstract ("This result suggests that designing sentencing guidelines that encourage strict judges to become more lenient could reduce recidivism") was removed from the latest abstract -- the policy recommendation is no longer in the abstract, likely moved to the body.

## Introduction Comparison
### Key Changes
1. **Opening sentence revised**: v1: "Understanding how different types of sanctions impact the behavior of defendants is a critical area of research in the field of Economics of Crime." Latest: "Understanding how different types of sanctions impact defendants' behavior is a critical area of research among Crime Economists." The revision is more concise ("defendants' behavior" vs "the behavior of defendants") and changes framing ("among Crime Economists" instead of "in the field of Economics of Crime").

2. **MTE framework attribution**: v1: "extending the marginal treatment effects (MTE) framework **developed by** Heckman and Vytlacil (1999, 2005)" became "extending the marginal treatment effects (MTE) framework **of** Heckman and Vytlacil (1999, 2005)" -- more concise attribution.

3. **Expanded discussion of bypassing censoring**: The latest version significantly expands the discussion of why researchers should not simply "binarize" the outcome (recidivism within 2 years). v1 devotes about one paragraph; the latest devotes approximately three paragraphs with a more detailed argument structure, including a new question-and-answer framing: "One question that naturally arises is whether empirical researchers can bypass these challenges..."

5. **LATE discussion reframed**: v1 had a long parenthetical about LATE limitations; latest separates this into a footnote: "To be fair, we stress that LATE was not meant to highlight this degree of heterogeneity..."

6. **Updated references**: "Giles (2021)" became "Giles (2023)"; "Possebom (2022)" became "Possebom (2023)" -- updated publication years.

7. **Expanded acknowledgments**: Latest adds seminar venues (Brandeis, Georgia Tech, SMU, Syracuse, U Georgia) and a research assistant acknowledgment.

8. **Section organization change**: v1's outline: "Section 2 describes the data and explains why focusing on long-term recidivism is useful..." Latest: "Section 2 describes the data and causal parameters and discusses why focusing on time-to-recidivism is useful..." -- clearer naming of content.

## Writing Style Patterns Observed
- **Sentence Structure**: Both versions feature long, complex sentences typical of applied econometrics papers. The revision tends to break long paragraphs into shorter ones and reorganize content for clarity.
- **Vocabulary/Terminology**: Shift from informal to slightly more formal/precise language ("in the field of Economics of Crime" to "among Crime Economists"). Capitalization conventions changed ("crime economics" to "Crime Economics").
- **Framing/Motivation**: The practical motivation is strengthened in the revision. The expanded discussion of why censoring matters and why binarization is inadequate shows a commitment to convincing practitioners.
- **Precision**: Grammar correction ("who" to "whom") indicates careful proofreading. Updated publication years show attention to bibliographic accuracy.
- **Tone**: Slightly more measured; removing "new" from "new econometric tools" and the policy recommendation from the abstract makes the tone less promotional and more academic.
- **Conciseness**: The abstract becomes slightly shorter by removing the policy recommendation sentence, but the introduction expands with more detailed argumentation.
- **Reader Engagement**: The literary epigraph from Les Miserables is retained in both versions -- a distinctive, humanistic touch unusual for econometrics papers. The latest version's expanded practical discussion of "should you binarize?" speaks directly to applied researchers.

## Specific Revision Examples

1. **Removing self-promotional "new"**:
   - v1: "This paper presents **new** econometric tools"
   - Latest: "This paper presents econometric tools"
   - *Why*: Lets the novelty speak for itself rather than declaring it. More confident by understating.

2. **Grammar correction**:
   - v1: "people **who** most judges would punish"
   - Latest: "people **whom** most judges would punish"
   - *Why*: Correct use of objective case "whom" when it is the object of "punish."

3. **More concise phrasing**:
   - v1: "Understanding how different types of sanctions impact **the behavior of** defendants"
   - Latest: "Understanding how different types of sanctions impact **defendants' behavior**"
   - *Why*: Possessive form is more compact and reads more naturally.

4. **Reframing for broader audience**:
   - v1: "In our view, the MTE framework is particularly attractive to studying the effect..."
   - Latest: "We find the MTE framework particularly attractive to studying the effect..."
   - *Why*: "We find" is more direct than "In our view" -- more confident, less hedging.

5. **Expanded practical argumentation**:
   - v1: Brief paragraph on why binarization is problematic.
   - Latest: Three paragraphs systematically dismantling the binarization alternative, including "One question that naturally arises is whether empirical researchers can bypass these challenges..." followed by structured analysis of the drawbacks.
   - *Why*: The expansion anticipates and addresses practitioner objections more thoroughly, making the paper more persuasive to its target audience.
